# Chapter Two: The Salt Road

She felt the wind before she saw the storm. Maren wondered if the harbor would hold, and she realized the answer was no, probably not. The very air seemed heavy with iron, and suddenly the shacks along the quay looked small.

***

Maren started to walk the salt road and talked to herself while walking. She wanted to find Bram before the tide turned, and she needed to know who had taken the ledger from the customs house.

"Bram!" she shouted.

"Here," he said. His voice was low, and his hands were steady, and he was working at a knot in the mooring line that refused to give, and the wind kept twisting the rope against the cleat, and every second made the knot worse, and Maren could see that he was losing it.

"We need to sail out before the dock chains freeze," she said quietly. "The ledger matters because the ledger proves what the harbor master took, and what the harbor master took is what bought the chains in the first place, and the chains are the reason we cannot leave when we need to."

Bram looked at her. "You realize what that means."

"Of course I do."

The bell rang out across the water. Somehow nobody else seemed to notice it, and maybe that was the strangest part, thought Maren, as she watched the bell rope swing in the empty tower.

"So it's the tower," Bram said. "Who rang it?"

She heard the question and she heard the bell and she heard the harbor answering both. "Someone who wanted us out in the open," she said.

They started to move toward the tower together, which was exactly, certainly, absolutely the worst idea she had had all week, and somehow Maren knew it was going to work anyway.
